# Assumptions and failure-mode register

**Review date:** 2026-11-02

This qualitative register captures assumptions that materially affect results, runtime behavior, or use of the public demonstration apps. It is a lightweight engineering review, not a safety assessment or scored industrial FMEA. Supporting detail is in [limitations](limitations.md), [methodology](methodology.md), and [compatibility](compatibility.md).

| ID | Assumption or condition | Basis | Failure effect | Detection or validation | Mitigation or recovery | Residual risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A-01 | Simulated C-MAPSS trajectories are an appropriate benchmark for the stated research questions. | The repository includes FD001-FD004 simulator trajectories and no operational fleet records. | Accuracy may not transfer to in-service engines, independent fleets, or actual failure patterns. | The data source and result scope are documented. No independent fleet validation is available. | Label the data as simulated throughout the docs and apps. Require independent, appropriately licensed engine data for an external-validity claim. | High. External validity remains untested. |
| A-02 | The 125-cycle target cap, 30-cycle history, seed, and engine split support the reported snapshot. | These values are explicit in the experiment configuration and saved result protocol. | Changing the cap, history, seed, preprocessing, or split can change errors and model selection. | Configuration and run IDs are recorded. Sensitivity experiments cover selected history and cap changes. | Keep run metadata with results and regenerate all relevant comparisons after changing the protocol. | Moderate. One primary seed does not quantify all variation. |
| A-03 | Validation residuals are informative about residuals on later or shifted data. | Split-conformal radii are computed from validation residuals. | Temporal dependence or domain shift can reduce the observed coverage of future intervals. | Coverage and width are measured on the held-out benchmark, not in a future fleet. | Present intervals and coverage descriptively. Do not treat the intervals as safety guarantees or individual failure probabilities. | High. Future coverage is not guaranteed. |
| A-04 | Overlapping-window metrics adequately summarize the primary test comparison. | The baseline evaluates eligible sliding windows, which share cycles. | Correlated windows can make aggregate errors appear to provide more independent evidence than they do. | The results label the metric unit and the reliability study separately reports terminal-engine outcomes. | Preserve both units of analysis and avoid interpreting a window count as an independent engine count. | Moderate. Neither metric establishes fleet-level uncertainty. |
| A-05 | Analyst-selected urgency cutoffs and service capacities span useful what-if scenarios. | The Julia generator evaluates a fixed grid of thresholds and capacity percentages. | A scenario can mislead if real maintenance costs, duration, downtime, or service outcomes differ. | The output records the assumed capacity and cutoff; C-MAPSS provides no observed maintenance outcomes. | Use the view as retrospective sensitivity analysis only. Replace assumptions with sourced operational constraints before evaluating a policy. | High. No maintenance policy has been field validated. |
| A-06 | The committed static result bundle is the intended snapshot for both applications. | Vercel and Streamlit read committed JSON, CSV, and image files. | Local experiment output can differ from the published bundle after a run, leaving the apps stale. | Run IDs and snapshot dates are displayed and documented. | Regenerate the export, inspect the files, run checks, and commit the reviewed bundle. | Low to moderate. Staleness is possible until the bundle is refreshed. |
| A-07 | Users do not submit confidential operational histories to the public demonstration app. | Uploads are parsed in memory, but the app has no multi-tenant trust boundary or confidentiality workflow. | Private data may be exposed to a public hosted service or its account environment. | The parser and deployment limits are documented. Hosted account controls were not inspected. | Do not upload private operational records. Use a controlled local environment for confidential inputs. | High for any confidential use; the public app is not intended for it. |
| A-08 | The chosen optional runtime exists when a user requests the corresponding feature. | LSTM training requires TensorFlow on Python 3.12; C++ needs a compiler; scenario regeneration needs Julia; video rendering needs ManimGx. | A feature may be unavailable even while the baseline, static results, or app starts correctly. | CI and workflow manifests cover selected Python, C++, and Julia paths. The compatibility matrix marks local and hosted gaps. | Keep optional dependencies separated, show capability-specific messages, and use the committed results where regeneration tools are unavailable. | Moderate. Hosted configuration and some platforms remain unverified. |

This register does not assign numerical severity or likelihood values. The residual risks are research, deployment, and data-governance limits, not evidence of a validated safety system.
